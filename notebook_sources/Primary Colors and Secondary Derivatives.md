# Primary Colors and Secondary Derivatives in Traditional Color Theory

## Introduction

Traditional color theory, rooted in the work of artists and color theorists from the 17th century onward, describes a system of colors based on pigments used in painting and printing. This differs from the additive color model used in light (RGB) and forms the basis of classical art education.

## The Three Primary Colors

In traditional color theory, the three primary colors are the foundation of all other colors. These colors are considered "primary" because they cannot be created by mixing any other colors together. They are:

1. **Red** — A warm, vibrant color that represents the first primary. Red cannot be produced by combining any other pigments. It is used as a base to create orange (with yellow) and purple/violet (with blue).

2. **Blue** — A cool color that serves as the second primary pigment. Blue cannot be made by mixing other colors. It combines with red to form purple and with yellow to form green.

3. **Yellow** — A bright, warm color that is the third primary pigment. Like red and blue, yellow cannot be produced by mixing other colors. It combines with red to make orange and with blue to make green.

These three primary colors — red, blue, and yellow — form the basis of the traditional RYB (Red-Yellow-Blue) color model used in art and design education.

## Secondary Colors

Secondary colors are created by mixing equal parts of two primary colors:

- **Orange** = Red + Yellow
- **Green** = Blue + Yellow
- **Purple (Violet)** = Red + Blue

## Tertiary Colors

Tertiary colors are created by mixing a primary color with an adjacent secondary color on the color wheel:

- Red-Orange, Yellow-Orange, Yellow-Green, Blue-Green, Blue-Purple, Red-Purple

## The Color Wheel

The traditional color wheel arranges colors in a circular format showing the relationships between primary, secondary, and tertiary colors. Complementary colors (those directly opposite each other on the wheel) create strong visual contrast when used together.

## Color Properties

- **Hue**: The pure color itself (e.g., red, blue, yellow)
- **Saturation**: The intensity or purity of a color
- **Value (Brightness)**: How light or dark a color appears
- **Tint**: A color mixed with white
- **Shade**: A color mixed with black
- **Tone**: A color mixed with gray
